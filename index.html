The Epochs of Indian Art

Understand how socio-political transformations, philosophical inquiries, and global cultural exchanges shaped five millennia of artistic creation.

3000 BCE – 500 BCE

Ancient & Harappan

Pioneered advanced urban planning, bronze lost-wax casting, terracotta fertility icons, and steatite seals. The Indus Valley Civilization demonstrated unprecedented technological finesse in metallurgy and ceramic art.

500 BCE – 600 CE

Classical Golden Age

Spanning the Mauryan, Satavahana, and Gupta empires. Marked by monumental polished stone pillars, Sanchi's narrative relief gateways, the sublime spiritual frescoes of Ajanta, and idealized Gupta sculpture.

600 CE – 1300 CE

Medieval Temple Dynasties

A period of extraordinary architectural courage: the monolithic top-down excavation of Ellora's Kailasa temple, Chola master bronzes (Nataraja), and the giant astronomical stone chariot wheels of Konark.

1500 CE – 1800 CE

Mughal & Regional Painting

Synthesis of Persian, Indian, and European traditions in imperial kitabkhanas. Gave birth to exquisite court miniatures and lyrical Pahari (Kangra) paintings celebrating devotional Vaishnava romance.

1800 CE – 1900 CE

Colonial Realism & Print

Raja Ravi Varma fused Western oil-on-canvas realism with Indian mythic themes, using chromolithography to democratize sacred imagery and establish visual standards for modern Indian iconography.

1900 CE – Present

Modern & Living Traditions

The Swadeshi Bengal School (Abanindranath Tagore), avant-garde modernism (Amrita Sher-Gil), and indigenous folk rituals (Madhubani, Warli) that remain continuous, living community art practices.
